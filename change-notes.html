What we changed, and why

Journey Open the assessment →

We have built your assessment form as a working prototype. In doing so we changed a number of things — not for design preference, but because the form as drawn either could not record the finding, could not be completed honestly, or would create a problem downstream. Every change is listed below with the reason. Each one also appears as an expandable purple CHANGE panel on the screen where it applies. Nothing here is final: tell us which changes you want reversed and we will reverse them.

We need four answers from you before implementation

  1. Range of motion — degrees or the 0–4 score? The prototype uses degrees; your original 0–4 scale is a one-line switch. See change #2.
  2. Sections 1 and 6–9 — your numbering references them but no images were supplied. We have drafted them. Please confirm or send the originals.
  3. Standardised outcome measures — are GMFM, GMFCS, MACS or PEDI in scope? Each is a scored instrument and a section of its own.
  4. Paediatric only, or adults too? Birth and developmental history only applies to children.
Changes that affect what the form can record
§10The Ashworth table has been rebuiltPlease review
Your formHeader reads Body Region | Left MAS Grade | Right MAS Grade | Notes, but each row pairs two different muscles — "Elbow Flexors" in column 1 and "Elbow Extensors" in column 3. The column labelled "Right" is therefore not the right side of the body.
PrototypeOne muscle group per row, with a genuine Left and Right grade. You choose which groups to load from a catalogue, exactly as in sections 11 and 12.
WhyIn cerebral palsy and hemiplegia the difference between the two sides is the finding — it drives which side is treated, which side is splinted, and whether Botulinum toxin is indicated. A table that cannot express left versus right cannot support that decision, and cannot show progress on one side.
§11Range of motion recorded in degrees, not as a score out of 4Decision needed
Your formEach ROM cell is a value out of four — 1/4, 2/4 and so on.
PrototypeDegrees, with the normal range printed under each motion and out-of-range values highlighted. Your 0–4 scale is retained and can be switched on by changing one setting.
WhyRange of motion is an angular measurement taken with a goniometer; there is no recognised 0–4 range-of-motion scale. A child whose hip abduction improves from 20° to 45° has made real, reportable progress — but on a 0–4 scale they stay at "2/4" and the improvement is invisible to you, to the referrer and to the family. You would also be unable to compare against normal ranges or produce outcome reports. If you want the 0–4 scale, we will keep it — but please decide deliberately, not by default.
§11Combined motions split into separate rowsApplied
Your formSingle rows for "Shoulder Flex/Ext", "Hip Flex/Ext", "Ankle DF/PF" — two opposite motions sharing one value.
PrototypeEach direction is its own row with its own normal range. The list is longer, but you only load the motions you actually tested.
WhyFlexion and extension have completely different normal ranges, and a child commonly has full flexion with a severe extension limitation — an equinus ankle is the classic example. One value for both hides exactly the finding being looked for.
§5, §3"Radio Selection" changed to multi-select with an exclusive optionApplied
Your formThe Respiratory row is marked (Radio Selection) — only one of Clear / Wheeze / Crackles / Secretions / Tracheostomy / O2 support may be chosen. Birth Complications lists "Nil" alongside real findings; Skin lists "Intact"; Vision/Hearing lists "Normal".
PrototypeMulti-select everywhere, with one option marked exclusive. Ticking Clear / Nil / Intact / Normal clears the rest; ticking any finding clears the exclusive option.
WhyA child can have a tracheostomy and secretions and be on oxygen simultaneously — that is a routine picture, not an edge case. A radio button forces the therapist to record one of the three, and the two dropped are usually the two that matter for handling the child safely. The exclusive-option rule keeps "Clear" and "Wheeze" from ever being ticked together.
§5, §10Muscle tone is no longer asked twiceApplied
Your formSection 5 asks Hypotonia / Hypertonia / Spasticity / Ataxia. Section 10 asks Overall Tone: Normal / Hypotonia / Hypertonia / Fluctuating / Mixed.
PrototypeSection 5 is a quick screen; section 10 is the source of truth and pre-fills from it. Contradicting the screen raises an amber note rather than silently storing two answers.
WhyTwo independent answers to the same clinical question will eventually disagree, and once they do neither can be trusted. Reporting also breaks: which field does a tone-distribution report count?
§5Neurological split into two questionsApplied
Your formOne row mixes Alert / GCS impaired with Hypotonia / Hypertonia / Spasticity / Ataxia.
PrototypeTwo questions: level of consciousness (single choice) and neurological findings (multi-select).
WhyThese are different clinical axes and a child has an answer to both at the same time. Mixed into one list, the therapist must choose between recording that the child is alert and recording that the child is spastic.
Changes that protect data quality
§10–12"Not Tested" with a reason on every measurementApplied
Your instructionAll fields in the grids are required.
PrototypeStill required — but a cell is satisfied by either a value or "Not tested" plus a reason (unable to cooperate, pain, fixed contracture, cast in situ, absent limb, refused…). Blank remains an error.
WhySome measurements genuinely cannot be taken. If the form refuses to accept that, therapists type a "1" to get past validation — and your database then says the child had severe restriction when in fact nobody tested them. An explicit "not tested, because…" is honest data that a reviewer can act on; a forced number is data that quietly misleads. This single change protects the credibility of everything else in sections 10 to 12.
§10–12Select first, then fill — instead of a fixed tableApplied
Your formFixed tables of 12–13 rows, most left blank on any given assessment.
PrototypeYou pick the joints or muscle groups you tested, and only those rows load. One-click presets — Upper limb, Lower limb, Spine, CP baseline, Full body — load a whole screen at once. This is what you asked for, applied identically to all three grids.
WhyA fixed table trains people to leave blanks, and a blank is ambiguous — was it normal, or never tested? Loading only what was tested makes every visible row meaningful, and makes a long, clinically correct catalogue (30 separate motions) practical instead of overwhelming.
§3Milestones interpret themselves, and allow "not achieved"Applied
Your formPlain number fields — "Head Control 35 month", "Walking 35 month".
PrototypeNumber of months, plus a Not achieved yet option, plus an automatic flag: Within range / Mild delay / Significant delay, measured against normal ages shown beside each field.
WhyTwo reasons. A child with cerebral palsy who has never walked cannot be given a number at all, so the original form has no valid answer for precisely the patients this department sees most. And "walking at 35 months" only means something to a reader who remembers that typical is 12 — the form should state it rather than rely on memory, particularly for junior staff.
§4Complaints, factors and goals are structuredApplied
Your formFree-text boxes for chief complaint, aggravating factors, relieving factors, and one box for all parent/child goals.
PrototypeCommon options to tap, with the free-text box still there underneath. Goals become separate rows, each with a priority.
WhyWith free text alone, "delayed milestones", "delay in milestone" and "milestone delay" become three different things and you can never report on your own caseload. And goals buried in one paragraph cannot be marked achieved individually at discharge — which is the one thing a rehabilitation service is judged on.
Changes for patient safety and governance
§2Allergies moved to the patient recordPatient safety
Your formA free-text "Allergies" row inside the rehabilitation assessment.
PrototypeSeverity-coded entries on the patient's permanent record — displayed and editable here, but stored once at patient level and visible to OPD, the ward and pharmacy.
WhyAn allergy typed into a physiotherapy form is invisible to everyone else treating the child. That is a patient-safety gap and a standard accreditation finding. Recording it once, at patient level, is the only safe design.
AllPrecautions follow the therapist through the formPatient safety
Your formA checkbox row at the bottom of section 2 — bone fragility, cardiac, respiratory, seizures, shunt, orthopaedic, skin integrity.
PrototypeWhatever is ticked appears as a red banner pinned under the patient name on every section and on the printed record. Bone fragility and Orthopaedic require a short note (which segment, weight-bearing status).
WhyA therapist about to mobilise a child with osteogenesis imperfecta or a VP shunt should not be relying on a checkbox they ticked eight screens earlier. This is the cheapest safety improvement in the whole form.
§1, §2, §7Fields owned by another department are read-only, with a source badgeApplied
Your formRows annotated "will be in opd" and "will be in rehab", with no visual difference between them.
PrototypeInherited fields render as read-only cards badged From OPD — Dr. Ahmed Raza, 18-Aug-2026, with a Request amendment action. Fields owned by rehabilitation are normal inputs.
WhyWhen the same field is typed in two departments the copies disagree within weeks and nobody knows which is correct. And a therapist silently editing the consultant's diagnosis destroys the record of who decided what — an amendment request keeps the audit trail intact while still letting the therapist raise the correction.
Sign-offDraft → Signed, with addenda instead of silent editsApplied
Your formNo attribution or sign-off block.
PrototypeTherapist name and licence number, date and time, supervising consultant, and a status that moves from Draft to Signed. After signing, corrections are recorded as dated addenda.
WhyA clinical record has to answer "who recorded this, when, and has it changed since". An editable-forever form cannot, and that is the first question an auditor — or a lawyer — asks.
Changes to how the form is used
AllFree navigation instead of a locked step-by-step wizardApplied
Original planA linear wizard: complete a step, press Next, repeat.
PrototypeA section list down the left with per-section status, free movement in any order, automatic draft saving, Back/Next still available for the guided path, and a single Complete assessment action that lists everything outstanding as clickable links.
WhyTherapists assess in whatever order the child cooperates, and frequently finish a form across two visits. A wizard that refuses to advance traps them on a section the child would not tolerate. Validation still happens — but at the end, all at once, where it can be acted on.
§11Pain is scored on an age-appropriate scaleApplied
Your formA Yes/No pain column in the ROM table.
PrototypeThe pain checkbox reveals a score, using the instrument matched to the child's age — FLACC under 3, Wong-Baker FACES for 3–7, Numeric 0–10 from 8.
WhyYes/No carries almost no information: mild discomfort and pain that stops the session look identical in the record. And asking a two-year-old to rate pain out of ten produces a meaningless number — the instrument has to match the child.
§12Method column removed; grades chosen, not typedApplied
Your formA "Method" column, annotated skip. Grades typed as free text.
PrototypeColumn removed as instructed. Grades are selected from valid MRC values including 4-, 4 and 4+.
WhyTyped grades arrive as "4", "4/5", "four", "4-5" and "gd 4". A dropdown makes strength data comparable between therapists and across visits.
AllPrinting designed in from the startApplied
Not in the originalNo print consideration.
PrototypePress Print anywhere in the assessment. The printed record shows only what was actually recorded — not forty empty checkboxes — with the patient header, precaution banner, signature block and page numbering.
WhyThe signed hard copy still goes into the patient file. Printing that reproduces the blank form wastes paper and buries the findings among unticked boxes.
§1, §6–9Sections we drafted because no image was suppliedNeeds your input
Your imagesSections 2, 3, 4, 5, 10, 11 and 12.
PrototypeSections 1 (Patient & Visit Details), 6 (Pain), 7 (Anthropometry & Observation), 8 (Reflexes & Reactions) and 9 (Sensory Assessment) drafted by us and clearly badged DRAFT.
WhyYour numbering jumps from 5 to 10, so these sections exist in your form but were not sent. Rather than leave gaps in a form you are reviewing, we have proposed what a paediatric neuro-rehabilitation assessment normally contains at those positions. Replace them with your originals and nothing else in the prototype changes.
